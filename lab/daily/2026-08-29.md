## What we learned

Practical identifiability did not survive uniformly even though structural rank
did. Stiffness remained the strongest parameter, but damping and especially
effective mass failed cross-target tail and bias gates.

The best direct-channel reference was worse than the coherently derived
position pipeline under nominal imperfections. Independent sensor latency and
timestamp offsets violated the same-time regression equation; deriving
kinematics from one stream and filtering force reduced this inconsistency.
However, centered Savitzky–Golay is an offline upper-bound diagnostic, not a
causal deployment solution.

Natural frequency was robust for some target classes but the modal
parameterization did not pass across all six. Effective mass should remain in
the model provisionally and must not be claimed as reliably identified.

## EXP-0001 ramp failure

The rigid ramp had `95.75%` DC energy, `0.148%` energy at/above target natural
frequency, acceleration RMS `0.00635 m/s²`, and inertial force only `0.696%` of
force RMS. Normalizing the regression columns made rank/condition appear good
but hid the acceleration column's poor absolute SNR. EIV attenuation then
pulled the mass coefficient toward zero, producing the recorded `-62.68%`
error.

## Failures / unexpected behavior

- No practical candidate passed. The best candidate missed damping/mass tail
  limits and all bias limits.
- The first unilateral unit assertion saw only floating-point sine residue at
  nominal zero crossings; the model deliberately snaps values below `1e-15 N`
  to exact zero, and the test was aligned with that declared tolerance.
- The exploratory force proxy produced tensile estimates during unilateral
  separation and failed badly, as the force-source comparison makes visible.
- Mild/no-direct-acceleration performance was not uniformly better than
  nominal because the coupled severity design changes noise, sample rate,
  latency, and filter/window discretization together. A one-factor experiment
  is required for causal attribution.

## Next step

Stay in Stage 1. Run a synchronized causal chirp experiment that varies noise,
rate, latency, and timestamp mismatch individually, then compares ordinary LS
with a transparent total-least-squares or instrumental-variable baseline. The
goal is to identify whether derivative noise, time alignment, or the mass
parameterization is the dominant blocker before adding estimator or vehicle
complexity.

### Results

- Timing: lightly damped `c` became practically biased at 1 ms displacement or
  acceleration offset. Force/filter-delay sensitivity crossed `c` and `m_eff`
  limits at 5 ms.
- Attribution: EXP-0002-like timing changed causal-low-pass mass error by less
  than one percentage point; derivative phase/noise remains the dominant mass
  problem.
- Pipeline: causal low-pass was the best overall causal compromise (about
  15 ms effective delay and 16.10 dB acceleration-noise attenuation). No causal
  pipeline matched the centered reference jointly.
- TLS: no general improvement.
- IV: mass improved strongly at 0.5–5 Hz, reaching 34.62% worst-target p95 and
  15.12% worst bias, but the high-mass target remained outside limits and
  damping failed under weak instruments.
- Classifications: `k` A, `c` B, `m_eff` B, natural frequency B, damping ratio C,
  inverse mass C.
- Parameterization: retain direct `[k, c, m_eff]`; ratio form was not supported.

### Interpretation

Timing explains a meaningful part of damping sensitivity, but correcting it
does not make the complete physical vector causally reliable. Frequency content
that increases nominal inertial information can also increase causal phase
error. Normalized condition numbers remained benign while mass estimates
failed, confirming that absolute acceleration and instrument strength must be
reported.

Stage 1 remains open. The next reduced-order test should require one globally
fixed, delay-compensated causal estimator to return a physical full vector; the
later controller's actual need for explicit mass must also be specified before
mass is either dropped or made a hard gate.

### Interpretation and next step

The sequential hypothesis failed. The covariance treated systematic

Stop tuning active `[k,c,m_eff]` identification on this architecture. The next
Stage 1 experiment should define the actual controller decisions and test
whether one low–mid chirp can recover decision-sufficient stiffness,
dissipation/resonance bounds, and risk classes without explicit accurate mass.
