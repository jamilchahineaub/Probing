## Why it was done

The purpose was to answer the smallest scientific question first: whether
stiffness, damping, and effective mass are separable from a small bounded
interaction before adding estimator complexity or vehicle dynamics.

## What we learned

The parameters are independently recoverable in every configured exact-data
finite window, but noise robustness varies strongly with excitation. Chirp and
multisine performed best in this development matrix. RLS and batch LS converge
to essentially the same final estimate because the model is stationary, all
samples are retained, and the RLS forgetting factor is one.

Full numerical rank is insufficient evidence of useful identifiability. The
compliant/sinusoid case reached parameter correlation `0.985`, while the
rigid/ramp case looked adequate for stiffness but badly underestimated mass.

## Failures / unexpected behavior

- Worst near-ideal error was `36.46%` for the rigid/ramp case, including a

  tuning either estimator.
- The first artifact smoke invocation exposed an indentation error in the CSV
  writer; the test suite had not imported the CLI module. The syntax was fixed.
- The next external-root smoke run exposed an assumption that all artifact

  both repository and external smoke roots.
- The installed SciPy 1.8.0/NumPy 1.26.4 mismatch remains; Milestone A avoided
  SciPy.

## Changes to our understanding

Confidence increased that the linear three-parameter problem is structurally
solvable under direct ideal measurements. Confidence did not increase equally
for arbitrary bounded probes: a ramp can be weak for mass, and high parameter
correlation persists even when the design matrix is technically full rank.
