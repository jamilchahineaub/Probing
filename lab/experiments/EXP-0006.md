# EXP-0006 — Passive ring-down safety observation

## Scientific question

> Does a zero-force causal observation period after the chirp provide enough
> ring-down information to eliminate or substantially reduce false-safe
> decisions without applying additional probing energy?

## Frozen protocol

- Preserve EXP-0001 through EXP-0005 and verify their artifact fingerprints
  before and after execution.
- Apply the same nonadaptive unilateral 0.5 N, 0.5–5 Hz chirp for 3 s used in
  EXP-0005, then command exactly zero force.
- Evaluate causal observation prefixes of 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, and
  3.0 s. No post-chirp excitation is allowed.
- Use the same target population, hidden 2 N sustained-contact maneuver, and
  SAFE/CAUTION/UNSAFE definitions as EXP-0005.
- Use disjoint training seeds 7101–7120, calibration seeds 7201–7210, and
  untouched validation seeds 8101–8120, with 12 targets per seed.
- Evaluate low, nominal, and high noise with synchronized causal-low-pass
  sensing. No centered or future-sample filtering is used in the primary
  result.
- Compare chirp-only, ring-down-only, chirp plus ring-down, and physical-
  diagnostic plus chirp plus ring-down features using the same transparent
  response-bound predictor.
- The causal ring-down features include envelope decay and fit quality,
  dominant frequency, logarithmic-decrement/damping diagnostics, residual
  amplitude, threshold dwell/time, zero crossings, and energy decay. Every
  feature uses only the prefix available at its decision time.
- A common conservative persistence rule changes SAFE to CAUTION when at least
  0.5 s has been observed, the measured decay is slower than the 2 s SAFE
  settling limit, residual displacement exceeds three noise standard
  deviations, and no more than 10% of the prefix is below both response
  thresholds. It can never promote a target to SAFE.

## Predeclared Stage 1 criterion

At least one fixed observation duration no longer than 2 s had to satisfy all
of the following on untouched validation data:

- nominal false-safe rate at most 1%;
- high-noise false-safe rate at most 2%;
- nominal and high-noise accuracy at least 80%;
- nominal and high-noise UNSAFE recall at least 90%;
- no added probing energy;
- every nominal EXP-0005 false-safe target detected as non-SAFE.

The three-class label definitions and thresholds were not changed after the
validation run.

## Reference run and integrity

- Run ID: `EXP-0006_20260829T194621.991365Z_s8101_2966d3d2`.
- Validation population: 240 targets — 158 SAFE, 34 CAUTION, and 48 UNSAFE.
- 20,160 held-out validation prediction rows across seven durations, three
  noise regimes, and four feature sets.
- All 12 integrity checks passed. Validation predictions were generated before
  the hidden sustained-contact response was simulated and joined.
- The passive command was identically zero. The median force-squared dose was
  `0.206124 N²s` at every duration; no validation or predecessor safety event
  occurred.
- EXP-0001 through EXP-0005 fingerprints were unchanged before and after the
  run. Eighty-one tests passed before untouched validation.

## Fixed-duration results

The zero-second row is an EXP-0006 chirp-only baseline fitted on the new seed
partitions; it is not a rerun of the frozen EXP-0005 model.

| Observation | Noise | Accuracy | False-safe rate | False-safe count | UNSAFE recall |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 s | Nominal | 89.17% | 2.44% | 2/82 | 89.58% |
| 0.0 s | High | 87.92% | 3.66% | 3/82 | 89.58% |
| 0.5 s | Nominal | 83.33% | **0.00%** | 0/82 | 91.67% |
| 0.5 s | High | 82.92% | **0.00%** | 0/82 | 89.58% |
| 3.0 s | Nominal | 84.58% | **0.00%** | 0/82 | 91.67% |
| 3.0 s | High | 84.58% | **0.00%** | 0/82 | 91.67% |

At 0.5 s, passive observation removed all held-out false-safe decisions in all
three noise regimes. It changed 9.17% of nominal decisions and 7.92% of
high-noise decisions. The trade was conservative: nominal SAFE recall fell
from 92.41% to 82.91%, largely because 15 additional SAFE cases became CAUTION.
No CAUTION or UNSAFE target was called SAFE.

Longer was not monotonically better for accuracy or settling prediction. Every
tested duration from 0.5 to 3.0 s retained zero false-safe decisions, but the
three-class confusion pattern varied. The 3 s fixed window met the nominal and
high-noise numerical accuracy, false-safe, and UNSAFE-recall limits, but it
exceeded the predeclared 2 s practical-duration limit.

At every fixed duration at or below 2 s, at least one of the nominal/high
UNSAFE recalls was 89.58% (`43/48`), one case below the 90% requirement. These
misses were UNSAFE-to-CAUTION, not UNSAFE-to-SAFE. The binary safe/not-safe
decision therefore succeeded while the stricter three-class Stage gate did
not.

## Feature and prediction findings

Nominal results at the selected 0.5 s reporting duration:

| Feature set | Accuracy | False-safe rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Chirp only | 89.17% | 2.44% |
| Ring-down only | 57.08% | 1.22% |
| Chirp + ring-down | 83.33% | **0.00%** |
| Physical diagnostics + chirp + ring-down | 83.75% | **0.00%** |

Ring-down is not sufficient by itself; it adds a conservative persistence cue
to the stronger chirp response. Adding separate estimated `c` and `m_eff` to
the combined set changed nominal accuracy by only 0.42 percentage point and
did not improve false-safe performance. Complete `[k,c,m_eff]` identification
is therefore not required for the observed safety benefit. Estimated
stiffness remains a useful chirp diagnostic.

Among ring-down variables, threshold dwell fraction had the largest absolute
standardized settling-predictor coefficient (`1.633`), followed by time to the
response threshold (`1.590`). These coefficient rankings are diagnostic, not
causal importances, because the decay features are correlated. The conservative
persistence veto uses the same physical idea: a large measurable residual that
has not spent enough time below threshold is not evidence for SAFE.

At 0.5 s under nominal noise:

- settling-time absolute error was `0.057 s` median and `0.778 s` p95, versus
  `0.079 s` and `0.939 s` for chirp only;
- peak-displacement relative error was `4.07%` median and `16.19%` p95;
- peak-displacement upper-bound coverage was `97.50%`.

The settling p95 improvement is useful but not a complete continuous-time
certificate: many actual values saturate at the 3 s evaluation horizon, and
the safe decision also relies on a conservative persistence rule.

## EXP-0005 false-safe audit

All five targets that were false-safe under at least one EXP-0005 noise regime,
including the three nominal false-safe targets, were reproduced only after the
EXP-0006 models and policy were frozen. By 0.5 s, every one was classified
CAUTION or UNSAFE in every noise regime, and the persistence veto was active on
the three nominal historical failures.

There is an important interpretation boundary: the newly fitted EXP-0006
zero-second baseline already classified these historical targets as non-SAFE.
They therefore validate that slow ring-down is visible and triggers the locked
rule, but they are not independent proof that ring-down corrected those exact
decisions. The untouched 8101–8120 validation population supplies the direct
before/after evidence: 2 nominal and 3 high-noise false-safe cases at 0 s,
versus zero at 0.5 s.

## Early stopping

The stopping rule used only prediction stability, response-bound margin,
decay-fit quality, and settling-bound change. Ground truth was unavailable to
the rule, and a unit test verifies invariance to altered truth fields.

| Noise | Accuracy | False-safe | UNSAFE recall | Median wait | p95 wait |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 80.83% | 0.00% | 89.58% | 1.5 s | 3.0 s |
| Nominal | 83.33% | 0.00% | 91.67% | 1.5 s | 3.0 s |
| High | 84.17% | 0.00% | 91.67% | 1.5 s | 3.0 s |

About 79% of low/nominal and 82% of high-noise cases stopped before the 3 s
maximum. The median wait was practical, but the p95 remained 3 s. Early
stopping is promising and causal, but it does not retroactively satisfy the
predeclared fixed-duration Stage criterion.

## Decision

Passive observation substantially reduced false-safe risk without added
energy: 0.5 s was enough to reach zero false-safe decisions in this held-out
population at low, nominal, and high noise. Slow-settling targets were reliably
kept out of SAFE, although one UNSAFE case remained conservatively labeled
CAUTION at high noise.

The predeclared Stage gate did not pass because no fixed duration at or below
2 s met the 90% UNSAFE-recall requirement in both nominal and high noise.
Stage 1 remains `CONTINUE_STAGE_1`; independent MATLAB/Simulink validation is
not authorized. Do not retune on seeds 8101–8120.

The next useful Stage 1 test is an independently seeded replication of the
locked 0.5 s passive persistence policy with a prospectively declared
hierarchical safety endpoint: SAFE versus non-SAFE as the primary actuation
decision, while preserving CAUTION versus UNSAFE as a secondary severity
classification. This tests whether the zero false-safe result generalizes
without changing the existing labels or tuning on this validation set.
