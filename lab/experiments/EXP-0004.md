# EXP-0004 — Sequential uncertainty-driven probing

## Scientific question

Can sequential, uncertainty-driven probing identify the complete target
dynamics more reliably than a single fixed probe while respecting the same
physical disturbance limits?

## Frozen method

The authoritative configuration is
[exp_0004_sequential_active_identification.yaml](../../configs/experiments/exp_0004_sequential_active_identification.yaml).
It fixes six target classes, held-out seeds 4101–4120, seven 1 N candidates,
five strategy families, parameter-specific causal estimators, a data-only
uncertainty rule, three-probe/duration/dose budgets, accuracy thresholds, the
matched-budget kill criterion, and the Stage 1 gate.

Development used seeds 1701–1705. Ground truth was not an argument to the
selection function and was used only to score completed choices. EXP-0001
through EXP-0003 artifacts were read-only and fingerprinted before and after.

## Main finding

Sequential probing did not improve complete-vector identification. The adaptive
strategy achieved `65.00%` full-vector success. A predefined fixed strategy at
the closest matched stage achieved `80.83%` with only `5.98%` command-dose
difference, and its worst-target p95 RMS error was lower (`25.67%` versus
`33.70%`). The kill criterion fired.

The strongest method was the initial fixed 0.5–5 Hz chirp itself: `90.83%`
success with 3 s duration and `1.482 N² s` dose. It still failed the strict
cross-target gate because rigid damping reached `32.13%` p95 and high-mass
mass reached `35.00%` p95.

## Why adaptation failed

The uncertainty diagnostic named damping weakest in every one of 139 follow-up
choices. Only `41.01%` of those probes reduced damping error; predicted
variance reduction and actual error reduction had `rho = -0.478`. The causal
filter and EIV errors are systematic and colored, whereas the covariance update
treated them as variance that more information would average away.

Confidence stopping was more reliable: 59 of 60 confidence-stopped trials met
all true thresholds. The continuation decision was poorly calibrated: 69 of 79
trials told to continue after probe one were already acceptable, and only
48.10% were acceptable at the end.

Nominal information per dose favored low multisine for stiffness and broad
multisine for damping/mass. This did not translate to estimator quality; the
single broad multisine had `315.80%` damping p95 and only `34.17%` full-vector
success. The practical best remains the low–mid chirp.

## Disturbance

Adaptive maxima were `1.0 N`, `15.68 mm`, `0.271 m/s`, and `5.323 m/s²`.
Median duration/dose/absolute input energy were 7.5 s, `3.149 N² s`, and
`0.0324 J`. No safety event occurred.

## Decision and next experiment

Do not tune this active full-vector architecture further and do not advance to
MATLAB/Simulink. The single most useful next Stage 1 experiment is a
decision-sufficiency test: determine whether one fixed 0.5–5 Hz chirp can
recover stiffness, dissipation/resonance bounds, and interaction-risk class
well enough for later safety decisions without requiring separately accurate
`m_eff`.

## Interpretation boundary

This is a held-out negative result for the tested one-dimensional causal
sensing/estimation architecture. It does not prove that every active
identification method is impossible, and it does not validate a UAV or
controller.
