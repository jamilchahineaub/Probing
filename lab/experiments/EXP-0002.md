# EXP-0002 — Realistic sensing and practical identifiability

## Scientific question

Does identification of `k`, `c`, and `m_eff` remain reliable when measurements
and contact assumptions become closer to what an aerial robot could obtain?

## Best practical candidate

Unilateral chirp, no direct acceleration, Savitzky–Golay velocity/acceleration
from position:

| Parameter | Worst-target p95 | Worst-target bias | Criterion |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `k` | 19.35% | 14.14% | 20% / 10% |
| `c` | 32.98% | 28.01% | 30% / 15% |
| `m_eff` | 62.58% | 36.75% | 30% / 15% |

All 60 candidate trials were full rank and stayed inside force/displacement
limits, but the error tails and biases failed. The low-mass target caused the
mass tail; the lightly damped target caused the damping tail; the rigid target
caused the stiffness tail.

At nominal severity this candidate's overall median/95th-percentile RMS errors
were 13.66%/27.95%. At severe severity they rose to 41.51%/79.11%. RLS did not
remove the bias and sustained convergence occurred in only 53.3% of cases.

## Errors-in-variables finding

For the best candidate, mean regressor-induced mass shifts relative to an
oracle-regressor fit reached +81.68% for the low-mass target and +75.34% for the
high-damping target. Large shifts sometimes partially cancelled force/timing
errors, so a modest final error in an individual case is not evidence that the
regressors are unbiased.

The design matrix remained rank 3. Practical failure therefore came from
signal scale, regressor noise, and cross-channel timing—not loss of algebraic
rank.

## Alternative modal parameters

Acceleration-form estimates of natural frequency and damping ratio were also
tested. The best candidate's worst-target p95 errors were 36.70% and 64.90%, so
this parameterization did not supply a cross-target rescue. Natural frequency
was much better for some target classes, but not enough to pass Stage 1.

## Contact and force findings

The simple unilateral model rejects tension and records separation intervals.
For the best pipeline, nominal unilateral performance was slightly better than
bilateral performance (27.95% versus 31.36% overall p95). The unilateral clamp
was therefore not the dominant blocker, though it is still not full gap/impact
dynamics.

The exploratory command-based force proxy clearly failed: median error 82.85%,
95th percentile 305.65%. It supplies no evidence for sensorless success.

## Why the EXP-0001 ramp failed

For the rigid target, the ramp was almost static: 95.75% of force spectral
energy was DC, 0.148% lay at/above natural frequency, acceleration RMS was
0.00635 m/s², and inertial force was only 0.696% of force RMS. Stiffness
accounted for 99.83%.

Thus the normalized regression could look well-conditioned after rescaling a
very small acceleration column. Its actual acceleration signal-to-noise ratio
was poor, and EIV attenuation pulled the mass coefficient toward zero. Chirp
provided roughly 153 times the acceleration RMS and sustained dynamic energy,
which explains its much stronger mass result in EXP-0001.

## Decision boundary

No MATLAB, Simulink, EKF, UAV, Gazebo, PX4, ROS 2, MPC, or Isaac work should
start. The most useful next test is a synchronized, causal chirp experiment
that isolates noise, sampling, latency, and timestamp mismatch one at a time
and compares ordinary LS with a transparent EIV-aware baseline.
