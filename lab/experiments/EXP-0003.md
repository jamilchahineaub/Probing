# EXP-0003 — Causal timing and EIV identifiability

## Scientific question

Are poor damping and effective-mass estimates mainly correctable timing,
filtering, and errors-in-variables effects, or are they too weakly identifiable
under the existing bounded probing architecture?

## Findings

Timing is important but not the dominant explanation for mass failure.
Lightly damped targets crossed the damping-bias threshold with only 1 ms of
displacement or acceleration offset. Force and filter-delay errors crossed
damping and mass thresholds at 5 ms. However, synchronizing the causal
low-pass pipeline left median mass error near 85%; EXP-0002-like timing changed
it by less than one percentage point.

Causal low-pass was the best overall causal compromise. It incurred about
15 ms effective acceleration delay while attenuating derivative noise by
16.10 dB. Trailing polynomial and alpha-beta-gamma estimates attenuated more
noise but introduced 30–40 ms effective delay and much larger damping bias. No
causal pipeline matched the centered offline reference jointly.

TLS was worse overall because standardized isotropic perturbations do not match
colored, correlated derivative errors. IV reduced mass error strongly in the
0.5–5 Hz causal-low-pass condition: worst-target p95 fell from 108.40% under
OLS to 34.62%, with five of six targets passing. The high-mass target had weak
instruments and remained outside the bias/tail limits. IV damping failed badly
for the lightly damped target, so IV is not a general solution.

Normalized condition numbers remained around 1.2–2.5 while mass errors could
approach 100%. Absolute acceleration strength mattered more: high mass produced
0.136 m/s² acceleration RMS and about 99% baseline OLS mass error; low mass
produced 2.663 m/s² and 13.68% error. Higher-band Fisher information did not
translate monotonically into better estimates because causal phase error and
instrument weakness dominated.

## Parameter classification

| Parameter | Class | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| `k` | A | all six targets under a low-band parameter-specific condition |
| `c` | B | four of six targets with 0.5–5 Hz causal low-pass OLS |
| `m_eff` | B | five of six targets with 0.5–5 Hz causal low-pass IV |
| `omega_n` ratio | B | target-restricted; one target invalid |
| `zeta` ratio | C | no practical fixed causal condition |
| inverse mass ratio | C | only three of six targets pass |

These are parameter-specific results. The winning `k`, `c`, and `m_eff`
conditions are not the same estimator, and the stiffness-winning TLS condition
does not yield a physical full vector for every target. No full-vector success
is claimed.

## Decisions

- Keep the direct `[k, c, m_eff]` parameterization. The ratio form worsened
  damping ratio and inverse-mass tails.
- Treat IV as a conditional mass diagnostic, not the new default estimator.
- Require relative timing uncertainty below 1 ms for later lightly damped
  interaction studies or explicitly include timing uncertainty in the model.
- Do not advance to MATLAB/Simulink. No single causal candidate passed the
  predeclared full-vector gate.

## Important limitations

- Exact one-dimensional linear target and simplified no-tension contact.
- Fixed common filters were intentionally not target-tuned.
- Centered Savitzky–Golay remains an offline reference.
- IV uses the known bounded command/contact activation history; unmodeled
  actuator/contact dynamics could weaken those instruments further.
- No weighted TLS covariance model was invented for correlated derived errors.
- No controller-necessity claim is made from this estimator experiment alone.
