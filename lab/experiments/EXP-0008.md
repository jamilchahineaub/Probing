## Frames and signs

- World frame: right-handed ENU, `+z` upward.
- Body frame: right-handed FLU, `+x` forward, `+y` left, `+z` upward.
- Attitude: scalar-first Hamilton `q_WB`; its rotation matrix maps body vectors
  into world coordinates. Euler XYZ angles are plots only.
- Baseline target normal: `+world-x`; positive contact force pushes the target
  in that direction, and the vehicle receives the equal `-world-x` force.
- Rotor numbering: front, left, rear, right. All thrust is `+body-z`; reaction
  signs are `[+1,-1,+1,-1]`.
- The rigid probe tip is `[0.30,0,-0.08] m` from the nominal CoM. Contact moment
  is evaluated in body coordinates as `r_probe × R_WB^T F_vehicle`.

## Vehicle, rotors, and controller

Nominal mass is `1.50 kg`; inertia is
`diag(0.029,0.029,0.055) kg m²`; arm length is `0.23 m`. Rotor laws are
`T_i = 1.90e-5*omega_i²` and `Q_i = 2.60e-7*omega_i²`, with 30 ms motor lag and
`0–900 rad/s` limits. Hover speed is `439.946 rad/s`, leaving substantial
thrust reserve. These are prospective symmetric research-quadrotor assumptions,
not values fitted to contact results.

The conventional cascade uses position/velocity feedback and a geometric
attitude/body-rate inner loop. Gains were fixed from nominal closed-loop
bandwidth/damping before contact trials. Measured normal force and known rigid
geometry provide an explicit contact-moment cancellation term, while the
contact wrench still enters the 6-DoF equations.

The normal contact controller combines bounded signed force feedforward, low-
gain force feedback, and an unloading-only admittance state with anti-windup.
This controller is an EXP-0008 baseline, not an optimized solution.

## Contact and target

The interface is a unilateral Kelvin–Voigt penalty with `5000 N/m` stiffness
and `10 N s/m` damping. Force is zero before contact, after separation, or when
the raw viscoelastic term would be tensile. Baseline friction is zero.

The moving structure retains the frozen Stage 1/2 equation

`F_contact = m_eff*x_ddot + c*x_dot + k*x`.

No target nonlinearity, multiple mode, hysteresis, plasticity, flexible probe,
or surface geometry complexity was introduced.

## Experimental design

The run contains 107 coupled physical configurations:

- seven canonical targets;
- 27 log-spaced structured `[k,c,m_eff]` combinations;
- 60 untouched log-Latin-hypercube targets from seeds 13101–13105;
- 13 one-factor perturbations of mass, inertia, vertical CoM, probe offset,
  contact angle, motor lag, and maximum rotor speed.

Each physical probe was evaluated with low, nominal, and high frozen sensing,
giving 321 decision rows. Predictions were computed before the subsequent
coupled sustained-contact outcome was attached.

## Failure mechanism

Coupling changed 33/107 binary physical labels relative to the reduced-order
2 N maneuver, including 20/94 target-population cases. Median reduced-order
settling time was 0.0 s, while median coupled settling time was 2.822 s.

Every one of the 19 nominal false-safe configurations failed only the coupled
settling threshold. None violated SAFE peak displacement, peak velocity,
late-hold oscillation, or contact-travel thresholds. All 19 were cases whose
binary truth changed when the later sustained maneuver became vehicle-coupled.

The passive ring-down remains measurable: the persistence veto fired on
21.50% of nominal decisions. Its scientific limitation is now clear. It
describes passive target decay after the small probe; it cannot foresee
oscillation continually injected later by an imperfect sustained-force
controller. This failure is distinct from the EXP-0005 target-only slow-decay
blind spot.

## MATLAB/Simulink cross-check

An independently written MATLAB equations implementation reproduced the stiff
representative case. Maximum Python/MATLAB differences were:

- contact force: `1.13e-11 N`;
- target displacement: `1.40e-15 m`;
- UAV normal position: `3.55e-15 m`;
- pitch: `5.03e-15 rad`.

The Simulink subsystem model executed hover equilibrium, offset-contact pitch,
and dynamically coupled UAV-normal/contact/target checks. MATLAB and Python
agreement means the failure is not attributable to one implementation's
integrator or sign convention.
