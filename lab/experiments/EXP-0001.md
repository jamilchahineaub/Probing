# EXP-0001 — Milestone A bounded interaction identification

## Scientific question

Can `k`, `c`, and `m_eff` of an unknown interaction target be identified from a
small bounded probing interaction under ideal or near-ideal measurements?

## Predeclared method

The authoritative configuration is
[exp_0001_interaction_identification.yaml](../../configs/experiments/exp_0001_interaction_identification.yaml).
It defines six target types, five unoptimized 1 N probes, perfect and near-ideal
measurement scenarios, development seed 1101, RK4 at 2 ms, batch least squares,
RLS, safety limits, and acceptance thresholds.

The estimator regression is

`F_measured = [x_measured, x_dot_measured, x_ddot_measured] [k, c, m_eff]^T`.

Rank and conditioning are computed after column normalization so units do not
dominate the observability diagnostic. The parameter-correlation matrix is
derived from the inverse normalized information matrix.

## Quantitative result

All seven acceptance checks passed. Every one of the 30 perfect-data
regressions had rank 3. Maximum perfect-data RMS relative errors were
`2.23e-13` for batch LS and `6.50e-12` for RLS. The near-ideal median was
`0.488%` for both.

The aggregate result hides an important failure: near-ideal mean error was
`13.082%` for the ramp, versus `3.849%` for half-sine, `0.698%` for sinusoid,
`0.136%` for chirp, and `0.272%` for multisine. The rigid/ramp case reached
`36.46%` RMS error, primarily because `m_eff` was underestimated by `62.68%`.

The largest perfect-data parameter correlation was `0.9850` in the
compliant/sinusoid case. Thus numerical rank 3 is necessary but is not evidence
of noise-robust identifiability.

No configured limit was exceeded: maximum force `1.0 N`, displacement
`16.20 mm`, velocity `0.2967 m/s`, and acceleration `12.04 m/s²`.

## Interpretation

EXP-0001 answers the question conditionally. In the exact linear model the
parameters can be separated over the finite response windows. With the stated
small independent sensor noise, chirp and multisine are robust in this matrix,
while a ramp is a poor effective-mass probe for the rigid target. RLS does not
improve the final stationary estimate over batch LS, as expected when both use
the same data and RLS has no forgetting.

This is not yet validation for unilateral contact, derived acceleration,
colored/bias noise, uncertain force input, nonlinear targets, or a UAV. No
probe has been optimized. The result uses development seeds and must not be
presented as held-out confirmation.

## Required follow-up before estimator expansion

- Repeat on untouched validation seeds.
- Sweep noise levels and sample period without changing the baseline estimators.
- Quantify errors-in-variables bias and sensitivity to acceleration estimation.
- Inspect the high-correlation sinusoid cases and weak ramp mass excitation.
- Do not move to EKF or a full UAV until these effects are understood.
